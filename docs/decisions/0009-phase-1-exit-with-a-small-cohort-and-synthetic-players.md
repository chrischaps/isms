# 0009 - Phase 1 exits with a small cohort and synthetic players

**Status:** accepted . **Date:** 2026-09-17 . **Session:** none (plan)

## Context
GDD 18 sets Phase 1's exit at "10-20 humans, 1-week epoch, retention & interviews", and TDD 18.4 builds towards it: S1.14 puts a society on a VPS over HTTPS with backups and mail, and S1.15's done gate is "budget assertions pass; epoch end produces an archive; 10-20 invite codes issued". Chris does not expect to be able to gather that cohort. The realistic players are Chris, perhaps one or two others, and programs. Meanwhile one day of Chris playing strategically found a run of defects the scripted householders never could (a rejection printed as `c41 already works at w19`, a price history from the wrong epoch, a job taken with no confirmation, an ask side that looked empty because it sold out each hour), because householders take a job only while unemployed, never switch, never found, never speculate.

GDD 11.4 (and its Q5 discussion) says fielding *extra* citizens through agents breaks one-person-one-citizen and contaminates the data of a canonical society. That stays true.

## Decision
1. **The exit criterion changes.** Phase 1 exits when: the S1.15 budget assertions pass; an epoch end produces an archive; the synthetic cohort of S1.16 has played at least one full epoch against a build with no open defect from its journals rated blocking; and Chris (with whoever joins him) has played a Freeport epoch and written down what it was like, in the terms of `docs/playtest/phase1.md`. Retention is dropped as a criterion: three people cannot measure it. The "felt difference" interview of Phase 2 is kept as the real human test of the project's thesis, with the same small cohort living a week in Freeport and then a week in the Commune.
2. **Synthetic players.** A new card, S1.16, builds a harness of LLM-driven players: each is an ordinary account with an API key, plays only through the public API (TDD 10.2's one-API rule; no engine hooks, no database access), holds a persona with goals, and keeps a journal of what it tried, what was refused and what it did not understand. They exist to find defects and to put strategic pressure on the economy, not to stand in for what a person feels. They are more patient and more literate than people and never quit, so they under-report first-ten-minutes friction; the human players' cold first sessions are the evidence for that.
3. **Where they may play.** Only in societies of a new class, `lab`. A `lab` society is never listed on `/public`, never counted in any cross-society comparison, and never canonical. GDD 11.4's rule is untouched for `canonical` and `community` societies. Each synthetic account's email is under `@agents.isms.test`, so telemetry and any later export can exclude them by construction.
4. **S1.14 is parked, not cut.** Nobody remote needs to reach a society yet; the server already catches up missed ticks, so a society on Chris's machine survives the machine being off. When a second human is real, a tunnel to that machine or a small VPS (a 2 vCPU / 4 GB box, as TDD 17 budgets) is a day's work. S1.14's provider-independent parts (the image, the backup and restore drill, `/metrics`) wait with it; S1.15 no longer depends on it.
5. **S1.15 is trimmed.** It keeps the epoch-end sequence, the archive (Phase 3 consumes it), the load test and its budget assertions, the `events` size measurement (T5), and the interview guide. It drops invite-code issuance, the mailed invitation back to the roster, and the bug bash "against production config" (there is none); the bug bash runs against the dev configuration and takes S1.16's journals as input.

## Consequences
The critical path to Phase 2 no longer passes through anything Chris must buy or configure. Order: S1.16, then S1.15 (whose bug bash reads the journals), then Phase 2's cards. The Phase 1 interviews that GDD 18 expected to reshape Phases 2-4 will be thin; the journals and Chris's own notes stand in for them, and the first real stranger's session, whenever it comes, should be treated as more informative than all of them. A `lab` class is one more value in `societies.class` and one filter on the public routes. Running the cohort costs API tokens: at one decision per agent per tick, an hourly society is about 170 calls per agent per week, and a 10-second dev society is bounded by a per-run budget the harness enforces.
